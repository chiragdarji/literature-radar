## What it shows

1. **Fetch strategy.** For 8 of 10 queries the app's search matches 84–100% of the gold
   papers, but it fetches only the 30 newest (PRD query rule 2: sort by publication date).
   With hundreds or thousands of hits in six months, the newest 30 are a thin, recent-only
   slice, so the papers PubMed ranks as most relevant almost never reach the ranker.
2. **Phrase search.** Multi-word queries are searched as an exact phrase (PRD query rule 1), so
   "tuberculosis vaccine" finds 28 papers against 466 for PubMed's normal search, and
   "dengue vaccine" 56 against 228. Papers that say "vaccines" or "vaccine against dengue"
   are missed.

## Caveat

If the app's fetch is changed to use Best Match, recall@30 against these Best Match gold
lists will rise partly by construction. recall@10 would then mainly test the app's own
ranking. A gold list reviewed by a scientist is the only fully independent yardstick.

PRD query rules 1 and 2 changed: each term also uses PubMed's own term matching, and the fetch
